A cube and a sphere

The same puzzle, twice. On the left the plastic you know; on the right the six cuts drawn on a sphere. Drag either one — they turn together, because they are the same object seen two ways.

Piece type = how many axes grip it. A corner sits where three faces meet, so three axes turn it. An edge belongs to two. A centre to just one. That count is the only thing that distinguishes the kinds of piece — and it is what the colours mean on both shapes.

Watch the outlined piece. It moves — but it always lands in a slot of its own kind: a corner into a corner, an edge into an edge. It could never fit anywhere else. And the sphere doesn't change at all, because the cuts land back on themselves.

Slide it. The cuts move, but the puzzle doesn't change — still 6 + 12 + 8. Every depth in this whole range builds the same 3×3×3. That band of depths is called a regime, and finding all of them is what the later chapters do.
