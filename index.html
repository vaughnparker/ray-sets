Ray-Sets

How many twisty puzzles could exist?

You can build an N×N×N cube for any N, so in one sense the answer is "infinitely many." But they all share the same canonical form. Strip a puzzle down to its mechanism and a surprising amount becomes finite — and countable. This is an interactive tour of that classification, from the axes of rotation down to the individual pieces.

Start here

One cube, up close

Hold a single puzzle first. A 3×3 has 26 pieces, and a piece's kind is just how many axes grip it — corner 3, edge 2, centre 1. Draw those axes as arrows, and one rule appears: rotate the cube and every arrow must land on another arrow, or it jams. That rule is what makes the whole classification finite.

Part 2 · elementary

How many regimes can a ray-set have?

Give each axis a single cut. The depth is a free continuous parameter, so there are infinitely many depths — but they collapse into finitely many regimes (distinct puzzles).
