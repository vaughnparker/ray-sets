Cut-regime strips — elementary systems

Each strip is one elementary axis system across cut depth (left = shallow, simplest · right = deep, most complex). A puzzle here is defined by its surface — the pieces you can see and solve. Wide cells are full-width regimes: puzzles you can build across a whole range of depths (cell width = that range). Thin outlined cells are zero-width regimes: distinct puzzles that exist at a single exact (usually irrational) depth, where the surface is momentarily unique — a piece pinched to a point (the dashed one is the degenerate cut straight through the centre). Hairlines are plain boundaries, where the surface doesn't change. Colour = surface piece count or piece type; hover any cell for its surface and total breakdown.

Why the tetrahedron has no depth-0 cell. The degenerate depth-0 cut (the dashed zero-width regime) appears for every system except the tetrahedron. A depth-0 collapse happens when opposite cut planes merge — the cuts x·u=d and x·u=−d become the same plane at d=0 — which needs the rays to come in antipodal pairs (as in the 3×3×3 → 2×2×2 collapse). The tetrahedron's rays have no antipodal partners — opposite a vertex is a face, not another vertex — so nothing merges at the centre, and its deepest regime simply runs all the way to the edge.
