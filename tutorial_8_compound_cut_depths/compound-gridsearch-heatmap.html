Cut-regime maps — compound systems

A compound axis system combines two elementary orbits, and each orbit carries its own cut depth — so the puzzle landscape is a 2-D square, not a strip. Each square below is one system: the x-axis is the first orbit's depth, the y-axis the second, both running shallow → deep (so the most complex puzzles sit at the top-right). Each cell is shaded by its total piece count — a smooth complexity landscape — and thick walls separate neighbouring cells that belong to different regimes, so a block of one shade with no internal walls is a single regime. Hover any cell for the two depths and the piece breakdown.

Two honest caveats. (1) This is a grid sample (N=20 per axis), so the regime count is a lower bound — regimes thinner than a grid line can be missed, and the degenerate depth-0 edges aren't sampled. (2) Unlike the elementary strips, these counts are by total pieces, not surface — matching the surface lens here is future work (it needs the flood-fill added to precompute_compound.py and a re-run).
